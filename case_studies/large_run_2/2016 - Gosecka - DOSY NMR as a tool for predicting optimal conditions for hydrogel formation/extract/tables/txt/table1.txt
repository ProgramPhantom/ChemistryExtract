**[Preceding Paragraphs]**
A single macromolecule of HbPGL can be approximated as a sphere-shaped nanoparticle coated with peripheral groups that are reactive toward boronic acids. The hydrodynamic diameter of a pure HbPGL macromolecule was calculated to be 4.36 nm ( D 5 7.92  10 2 11 m 2 /s), which was consistent with the value obtained from GPC analysis (3.84 nm) using a RALLS detector. The corresponding volume of a single HbPGL macromolecule was 43.4 nm 3 .

Because the reaction equilibrium of the formation of cyclic boronic esters is strongly dependent on the solution pH, 12 we demonstrated the effects of pH on the molecular size of HbPGL aggregates [Fig. 3(a)]. For the reactions with benzene-1,4-boronic diacid, these effects were readily apparent. At an 11.0:1 molar ratio of diol groups to boronic diacid, that is, 5.5 diol groups per reactive boronic acid site, we analyzed the effects at pHs from 10 to 13 (above the p K a of the boronic acid; Table 1). For DOSY NMR investigations, the weight concentration of the polymer was kept constant at 66 mg/mL. The diffusion coefficient values varied from 6.17  10 2 11 at pH 10 to 5.11  10 2 11 m 2 /s at pH 13. Consequently, the average molecular volume of the diffusing objects gradually increased from 92 nm 3 at pH 10 reaching 163 nm 3 at pH 13. Additionally, when the molar ratio of the diol functional groups per boronic acid site was 2.75:1, the diffusion coefficient changed from 5.87  10 2 11 m 2 /s at pH 10 to 3.86  10 2 11 m 2 /s at pH 13, which corresponds to aggregates volumes of  107 and 377 nm 3 , respectively. These results indicate that the hydrogel formed at pH 13 would have the highest cross-linking density and the best mechanical strength. At this pH, we also examined the influence of the BDBA concentration on the efficiency of cross-linking. Our studies showed that the BDBA concentration had significant effects on the sizes of diffusing objects. Generally, the trend was as follows: the lower the concentration of BDBA, the smaller the molecular sizes of the aggregates [Fig. 3(b)]. For example, in a sample with the highest cross-linker concentration (i.e., 2.75 mole diol groups per boronic acid site), the diffusion coefficient was significantly reduced, compared with that of the pure polymer, to 3.86  10 2 11 m 2 /s, which corresponded to an average hydodynamic volume of 377 nm 3 diffusing objects (hydrodynamic diameter 5 8.96 nm). When the polymer and BDBA were mixed at a diol group to boronic acid site molar ratio of 10.5:1, the diffusion coefficient was 6.57  10 2 11 m 2 /s, and the aggregate volume was only 76 nm 3 ( d H 5 5.26 nm). The increased diameters of the diffusing objects resulted from the increased number of diester connections formed per macromolecule, which is due entirely to the increased amount of BDBA. Based on a detailed analysis of the 1 H NMR spectra, after signal deconvolution, the absolute concentration of boronic diester was calculated and the number of cross-links per HbPGL macromolecule was estimated. For example, when the molar ratio of diol to boronic acid site was 2.75, more than three crosslinks per HbPGL macromolecule were indicated, and the volume of each polymer aggregate was 377 nm 3 . When the molar ratio was 5.50, the hydrodynamic volume of each diffusing object was 163 nm 3 , and the estimated number of cross-links per macromolecule was two. Additionally, for the initial 10.5 molar ratio, one boronic cross-link was generated per macromolecule, and the hydrodynamic volume of each polymer aggregate was only 76 nm 3 . This can be attributed to HbPGL dimers. The increase in the polymer aggregate size with increasing boronic diester concentration indicates that for specific polymer concentrations selected for DOSY NMR measurements, intermolecular cross-linking plays a predominant role.

The rheological characteristics (based on dynamic viscoelastic measurements) of the hydrogels prepared under corresponding DOSY NMR conditions (i.e., the same pH and diol to cross-linker molar ratios but higher concentration at 242.4 mg/mL of polymer) confirmed the validity of the DOSY NMR results in predicting the most and least favorable conditions of gelation. G 0 ( x ) &gt; G 00 ( x ) at high angular frequencies confirmed network formation in water (see Supporting Information Figure S7). The plateau modulus G 0 max( x ) at high frequencies, which is proportional to the total number of association sites, gradually increased with pH and exceeded 6  10 5 Pa at pH 12 and 13. Under these conditions, the largest aggregates were observed with DOSY NMR. Consequently, the experimental number density of elastically effective network chains ( N E,exp) also increased 18 [Fig. 4(a)]. The differences in storage modulus maximum (in the plateau region) for hydrogels prepared at various pH were not significant (e.g., 1  10 5 Pa at pH 10 to around 7  10 5 Pa at pH 13). However, with increasing pH, the angular frequency (i.e., the width of the plateau) 19 significantly shifted at the G 0 and G 00 crossover (where G 00 was at a maximum) to a lower value, that is, from x c (pH 10) 5 99.5 rad/s to x c (pH 13) 5 2.2 rad/s (Fig. 5). The crossover point corresponded to the gelation point, that is, having both a liquid and solid-like behavior. For transient networks, this parameter describes the lifetime ( s ) of the gel forming bonds ( x c 5 1/2 p  s ). 19 In our case, this parameter is highly pH-dependent. Lower values of x c correspond to longer bond lifetimes. Rheological experiments showed that network formation occurred over the entire pH range at room temperature and the applied polymer weight fraction. However, at pH 10 and 11, liquid-like behavior was predominant, whereas at pH 12 and 13, gels were more solid-like. The G 0 max ( x ) of the hydrogels prepared at pH 12 and 13 were similar.

At pH 13, we prepared a series of hydrogels with different concentrations of BDBA. An increased molar concentration of BDBA resulted in a significantly increased G 0 max [Fig. 4(b)] due to an increased cross-linking density between individual macromolecules. These observations agreed with the DOSY NMR results. The G 0 max ( x ) for the hydrogel at the lowest cross-linker concentration (10.6 diol groups per boronic acid site) was equal 4.5  10 3 Pa, whereas for a diol to acid molar ratio of 2.75:1, the G 0 max ( x ) exceeded 10 6 Pa. We expected this trend because a similar trend was observed for semidiluted solutions where the diffusion coefficient of HbPGL varied from 6.57 to 3.86 10 2 11 m 2 /s, respectively.
