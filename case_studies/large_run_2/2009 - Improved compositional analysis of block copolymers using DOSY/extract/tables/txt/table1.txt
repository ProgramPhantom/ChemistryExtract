## 4. Results and discussion

As a proof of principle, we selected a series of polystyrene/ poly(ethyleneoxide)amphiphilicblockcopolymer(PSb -PEO)samples which are industrially significant because of their remarkable biological and chemical properties. These polymers can form highly ordered, self-assembled nanostructures that have been recently used as mesoporous silica films of different pore sizes [20] and electrolytes for rechargeable batteries [25]. Specifically the block PSb -PEO samples under study were obtained by controlled freeradical nitroxide mediated polymerization (NMP) [26]. Indeed, NMPisanefficient technique that has recently attracted increasing interest because it allows the polymerization to occur in a living fashion with a high degree of control over the entire polymerization process [20]. In our case, the macroinitiator was obtained as described in Ref. [27] by functionalizing a well-characterized, monodisperse (polydispersity index I p &lt; 1.1) PEO homopolymer sample. All PSb -PEO copolymers samples were batch fractionated by using selective precipitation with ethanol. DOSY experiments were then performed on dilute CDCl3 solutions of these samples. Specifically, in a DOSY map, the NMR signals of a given set of nuclei are correlated with the diffusion coefficient of the molecule to which they belong, provided that no intermolecular interactions [28] or chemical exchange are present [29,30]. The data inversion step undoubtedly represents the key to DOSY, as it is typically impossible to guess a priori the number of components to fit when analyzing unknown mixtures. Moreover, to simulate the result of an unsuccessful or incomplete fractionation, a small amount of the end-functionalized PEO homopolymer was added to the copolymer solutions, and the resulting samples were also analyzed by DOSY. As an illustration, results obtained for one of these PSb -PEO samples are reported in Fig. 1. Note that the NMP was stopped to reach a PS block characterized by an average molecular weight of about 10 kDa. Therefore, a dilute CDCl3 solution of a monodisperse PS homopolymer ( M n 10kDa) was also analyzed by DOSY for comparison. Fig. 1a and b show the results of the DOSY experiments recorded on dilute CDCl3 solutions of the PS and the end-functionalized PEO homopolymer samples, respectively. The 1 H signals due to the PS sample, resonating both in the 7.2-6.3 ppm and 2.0-1.3 ppm spectral regions, are all aligned on the same horizontal line. Similarly in Fig. 1b the 1 H signal due to the PEO sample (3.6 ppm) is characterized by a larger diffusion coefficient. In parallel, Fig. 1c and d show the results obtained on dilute CDCl3 solutions of the PSb -PEO block copolymer before (Fig. 1c) and after (Fig. 1d) complete batch fractionation. Clearly, Fig. 1d shows that the 1 H PEO signal (evidenced by the dashed circle) is perfectly aligned on the horizontal line characterizing the 1 H PS signals. This implies that all these 1 H signals are due to the same macromolecule whose diffusion coefficient is lower than that of the PS and PEO homopolymers. Therefore DOSY indicates that the copolymerization reaction was carried out successfully and that only the PSb -PEO copolymer is present in solution. In this case, because the above mentioned 1 H signals are strictly due to the PS and PEO blocks of the PSb -PEO copolymer, careful integration of these signals in the corresponding 1 H spectrum can accurately reveal the M n value of the PS

On the contrary, Fig. 1c shows that the PS and PEO 1 H signals are not characterized by the same diffusion coefficient. Performing the compositional analysis on a sample characterized by such a DOSY map would clearly lead to an incorrect composition (the M n value of the PS block will be overestimated). A finer analysis of the PEO signal decay in the PGSE experiment using a biexponential function allowed us to obtain for this signal two distinct diffusion coefficients: 1.32 × 10 -10 m 2 s -1 and 3.21 × 10 -10 m 2 s -1 (Table 1). Within our experimental error ( ± 4%), the former corresponds to that of the PSb -PEO copolymer, which presence in solution was hence confirmed, whereas the latter corresponds to that of the unreacted end-functionalized PEO homopolymer. In other words, the position of the PEO correlation spot in the simplified DOSY processing showed in Fig. 1c echoes the presence of both species. In principle the amounts of the two components obtained from the biexponential fitting would allow the relative concentration of the two species to be estimated. Indeed, these proportions could be used to recalibrate the integral of the PEO signal (used as a reference) in the corresponding quantitatively recorded 1 H NMR spectrum. However, because of possible complications arising from differences in the magnetic relaxation properties of the involved polymers, care should be taken when applying such method, which should only be regarded as a way to achieve a semi-quantitative estimation. Nonetheless the progress of the purification/reaction can already be fairly evaluated from the position of the PEO spot, which is expected to be closer to that of the PS peaks as the relative copolymer concentration increases.
