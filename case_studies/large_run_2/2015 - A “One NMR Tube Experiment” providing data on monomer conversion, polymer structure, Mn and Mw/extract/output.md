## L-Lactide polymerization studied by 1 H NMR with di ff usion-ordered spectroscopy (DOSY): a ' One NMR Tube Experiment ' providing data on monomer conversion, polymer structure, M n and M w †

P. Lewinski, S. Sosnowski,* S. Kazmierski and S. Penczek*

L-Lactide polymerization was studied by a ' One NMR Tube Experiment ' . Traditional 1 H NMR, which provides structural information, conversion values, and number average molar mass ( M n), was combined with di ff usion-ordered spectroscopy (DOSY), yielding a mass averaged molar mass ( M w). Thus, a complete kinetic experiment could be performed without sampling, and hence in one NMR tube.

Di ff usion-ordered spectroscopy (DOSY) was first described fifty years ago, and today every NMR spectrophotometer has the capability to conduct DOSY experiments. 1 DOSY gives the dependence of the intensity of the peak ascribed to given nuclei on their di ff usion coe ffi cient ( D ) and magnetic field gradient. 1 The physical principle of DOSY has been explained clearly in the literature and various aspects of the experimental set-up have been discussed. 2 -4 An exhaustive review of original papers devoted to the method and its applications in various areas of organic chemistry, including polymer chemistry, has also been published. 5 In polymer chemistry, DOSY has mostly been used to determine M w. We here describe our application of this method to the polymerization of L-lactide (LA), to complement our kinetic work.

$$R _ { H } = K M ^ { \nu }$$

In this simple equation, which resembles the Mark -Houwink -Sakurada equation, 10 K is the proportionality constant and ν is the scaling exponent.

Finally, the relationship between M and the di ff usion coe ffi cient has a relatively simple form (3):

$$D = K ^ { \prime } M ^ { - \nu }$$

The coe ffi cients K ′ and ν have been determined for a few polymer-solvent systems. 7,11 -13

Recently Grubbs et al. applied DOSY for the first time in studies of a controlled polymerization of styrene and of 1,5cyclooctadiene (ROMP). 13 The authors acquired the monomer conversion and mass average molar mass ( M w) from analyses of samples taken from the reaction mixture.

We decided to follow the approach used by Grubbs et al. in our study of the polymerization of L-lactide, although in place of sampling the reaction mixture we performed polymerization in a sealed NMR tube, acquiring all the information ' on-line ' without taking samples. This was possible because the rate of the studied process is su ffi ciently slow at the measurement temperature, assuring that neither the measurements nor the polymerization were disturbed.

This work was divided into three parts: first, the dependence of the di ff usion coe ffi cient on M w for poly(L-lactide) (PLA) was established; then, this dependence was verified in a kinetics experiment involving sampling the polymerization mixture; and finally, the ' One NMR Tube Kinetic Experiment ' was carried out. All experimental details, analytical procedures, and results of the characterization of the synthesized samples are described in ESI. †

Five samples of PLA were prepared, by polymerization with an Sn(2-ethylhexanoate)2 (Sn(Oct)2)/benzyl alcohol (BzOH) catalytic system, and precipitated from the polymerization solution. M w values were measured by size exclusion chromato- graphy (SEC) and D using DOSY. In addition, number average molar masses ( M n) were determined using 1 H NMR and SEC, and compared with M n calculated from the conditions used for polymerization. The tabulated (Table 1) data were used to determine the analytical dependence of D on M w (Fig. 1). Even though the established dependence is valid only for the polymer concentration of 0.5 g l -1 , this dependence was successfully applied in studies of polymerization kinetics with di ff erent concentrations of the formed polymer by determining correction factors. Changes in the polymer concentration may also influence the di ff usion coe ffi cient by changing aggregation phenomena or simply by changing the viscosity of the solution. In order to correctly analyse the experimental data, the dependence of D on polymer concentration (from 0.5 to 30 g l -1 ) was determined and plotted, as in Fig. 2.

These relationships were found to be linear, with their slopes not dependent on molar masses. The mean value of the slope ( S ) was calculated to be (1.3 ± 0.2) × 10 -12 m 2 l g -1 s -1 and is the same for all molar masses within the studied range. Thus, the corrected value of the di ff usion coe ffi cient can be calculated from eqn (4).

$$D _ { c o r t } = D _ { e x p } - S ( c _ { e x p } - c _ { c o r t } )$$

In this equation, c is concentration, and the indices ' exp ' and ' corr ' denote experimental and corrected values, respectively. Finally, the M w values were calculated from the D corr using the equation given in Fig. 1.

The monomer conversion and degree of polymerization P n were determined from integrations of the standard 1 H NMR spectrum (Fig. 3). The spectrum shows the structure of active macromolecules being in equilibrium with the temporarily inactive chains with -OH end groups, in agreement with ref. 15. The monomer conversion was calculated from the ratio of the integration of the signal from methine protons of the lactoyl units e (overlapped quartets at δ = 5.0 -5.1 ppm) to the integration of the monomer signal (quartet centred at δ = 3.75 ppm). Signals e ′ and g broadened due to an equilibrium between active and inactive chains and therefore cannot be used for calculations of P n of living macromolecules. P n and M n were calculated from the ratio of the integrations of signals e and d ′ (the latter at δ = 4.89 ppm).

## Conclusions

It has been shown, in the controlled polymerization of L-lactide, that using a combination of di ff erent NMR techniques (standard and DOSY) allows studying on-line the kinetics of polymerization together with the determination of M n, M w and Đ .

Moreover, in the same experiment carried out in a single NMR tube, it was possible to analyze the structure of poly(L-lactide).
