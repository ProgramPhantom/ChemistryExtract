Hydroxyethyl starches (HESs) are efficient plasma volume expanders in infusion therapy; their basic function is the completion of the intra vascu/hyphenminus lar volume of liquid [1, 2]. The molecular structure of HESs represents a polydisperse branched amy/hyphenminus lopectin, an α /hyphenminus D /hyphenminus g l ucopyranose polymer with sub/hyphenminus stituted  hydroxyethyl  groups.  The  linear  part  of HES macromolecules contains α (1 4) bonds and  branches,  mainly α (1 6) bonds  (see scheme).

Starches of natural origin cannot be used as plasma volume  expanders,  because  they  are  rapidly  hydro/hyphenminus lyzed under the action of amylase. Substituted hydrox/hyphenminus yethyl  groups  in  the  HES  molecule  decelerate  the hydrolysis and, correspondingly , the metabolic degra/hyphenminus dation and elimination of preparations from the blood flow [5]. The  physical and chemical properties, metabolism, and excretion of HESs depend not only on the properties acquired in the hydroxyethylation of starch,  but  also  on  its  molecular  weight  distribution (MWD). On the introduction of a polydisperse colloi/hyphenminus dal solution of HES into the blood circulatory system, small molecules with the sizes smaller than the value of kidney threshold (45-60 kDa) were quickly excreted, whereas larger molecules remained in the blood circu/hyphenminus latory system for a long time, depending on their size and rate of decomposition [1]. Therefore, the determi/hyphenminus nation of MWD is an obligatory task in the pharmaco/hyphenminus poeia analysis of the quality of HES medicinal sub/hyphenminus stances [6].

The main parameters of the MWD of polymers are the average molecular weight (MW) and polydispersity index,  which  characterizes  the  statistical  width  of MWD. The average MWs of HES/hyphenminusbased preparations present in the market vary in range 70-670 kDa [7]. Depending on the averaging method, we can distin/hyphenminus guish the weight/hyphenminusaverage molecular weight M w (aver/hyphenminus aging by the weight of macromolecules in the polymer) and the number/hyphenminusaveraged molecular weight M n (aver/hyphenminus aging by the number of macromolecules in the poly/hyphenminus mer). The value of M w is  sensitive to high/hyphenminusmolecular fractions and that of M n , to low/hyphenminusmolecular fractions of polydisperse polymers. Therefore, the average MW of polymers is often characterized by molecular weight at the  maximum  of  the  chromatographic  peak М p ;  for narrow/hyphenminusdisperse samples with close values of MWs of macromolecules, М p = ( M w × M n ) 0.5 [8].  The M w / M n ratio determines the polydispersity index of a polymer, from which one can estimate the molecular weight dis/hyphenminus persion of the macromolecules and find the number of branching sites in a branched polymer macromolecule [8].  A  decrease  in  the  polydispersity  index  of  HESs improves the pharmacokinetics of the preparation and reduces its effect on the rheological properties of blood after the infusion [9].

Now the safest method of the determination of the average MW and polydispersity index of polymers is provided by the method of multidetector gel perme/hyphenminus ation  chromatography  (GPC)  [10].  Gel  permeation chromatography with a light scattering detector and a concentration detector including a viscometer ensures the determination of molecular masses irrespectively of  the  method  of  column  calibration.  In  the  last decade, a new promising method has been developed for the determination of the average MW of polymers, i.e., diffusion/hyphenminusordered NMR spectroscopy ( DOSY ). It ensures measurements of the self/hyphenminusdiffusion of various molecular objects (molecules, macromolecules, molecular complexes, supramolecular systems) using a magnetic field gradient [11]. The DOSY method is based on the registration of the loss of phase coherence among  nuclear  spins  because  of  the  translational motion of molecules in this field [12, 13]. The data on diffusion  are  used  for  the  quantitative  assessment  of the  sizes  of  polymer  macromolecules  and,  corre/hyphenminus spondingly, their molecular weights  [14-20].  An advantage of the DOSY method is that, along with the diffusion  data,  it  also  provides  spectral  information (values  of  chemical  shifts,  multiplicity  of  signals), which allows the researcher to solve a whole range of problems of the pharmacopoeia analysis of the quality of HES medicinal substances, namely, of the confir/hyphenminus mation and quantitative assessment of molar substitu/hyphenminus tion (ratio of the number of hydroxyethyl groups to the total  number  of  glucose  molecules)  and  the  C2/C6 ratio (the ratio of the number of hydroxyethyl groups at the second carbon atom to the number of hydroxy/hyphenminus ethyl groups at the sixth carbon atom in the glucopyr/hyphenminus anose cycle) within one experiment.

In the DOSY method, a calibration dependence of the parameter of MWD on the coefficient of self/hyphenminusdif/hyphenminus fusion D (a quantitative characteristic of the diffusion processes) is determined for a series of objects similar to the studied system in nature and then extrapolated to the value of D of the studied object. In determining the correlation equation, a quite important problem is the  correct  interpretation  of  the  data  of  a  DOSY experiment, because the determination of the value of D for a polymer as a whole is a nontrivial task even if all instrumental sources of errors are considered and the values of D are corrected. The main difficulty is associ/hyphenminus ated with the fact that not all nuclei in the polymer mac/hyphenminus romolecule are characterized by single values of D .

The aim of this work consisted in a comparison of different methods for the determination of the coeffi/hyphenminus cient  of  self/hyphenminusdiffusion D of  HES  macromolecules  as whole and in the determination of a functional depen/hyphenminus dence between the main MWD parameters of HESs and D .

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

By self/hyphenminusdiffusion is meant the spatial movement of molecules in a medium under a thermodynamic equi/hyphenminus librium due to chaotic thermal movement [23]. Self/hyphenminus diffusion is characterized by the coefficient of self/hyphenminusdif/hyphenminus fusion D ,  which  is  numerically  equal  to  the  root/hyphenminus mean/hyphenminussquare  displacement  of  a  molecule  within  the diffusion time t d . In the DOSY method, the data on the value of D for  a  molecular system are obtained from the analysis of diffusion decay , the dependence of the spin echo signal amplitude on the parameters of mag/hyphenminus netic field gradient, described by the Stejskal-Tanner function [24]:

$$I ( \delta _ { p u l s } , \Delta ) = I _ { 0 } \exp [ - D \gamma ^ { 2 } g ^ { 2 } \delta _ { p u l s } ^ { 2 } ( \Delta - \delta _ { p u l s } / 3 ) ] ,$$

where I and I 0 are spin echo signal amplitudes in the presence and absence of a pulse of magnetic field gra/hyphenminus dient; ∆ is  diffusion time, ms; δ puls is  the duration of gradient pulse, ms; γ is the nuclear gyromagnetic ratio, Hz T -1 ; and g is magnetic field gradient, T m -1 .

It is known that the dependence of D on the size of molecular objects is described by the Stokes-Einstein equation [25]:

$$D = k T / 6 \pi \eta R _ { h } ,$$

where k is the Boltzmann constant, J К -1 ; Т is absolute temperature, K; η is the viscosity of solution, Pa s; and R h is hydrodynamic radius, m.

Polymer solutions are usually characterized by sev/hyphenminus eral  values  of D ,  even  for  narrow  MWDs,  when  the sample consists of a set of macromolecules similar in lengths  and  weights.  One  of  the  reasons  for  this  is existence  in  of  a  number  of  states  corresponding  to minima in the potential energy surface of the studied system (for example, different conformations or asso/hyphenminus ciates  with  the  solvent)  and  a  chemical  exchange between them (spin transition or transition of a group of  spins  between  several  states).  In  the  case  of  slow chemical exchange, each set of signals corresponding to  different  states  will  be  characterized  by  a  certain value  of D .  Another  reason  for  the  experimentally observed dispersion of D values of nuclei in a polymer molecule are different local mobilities of segments of the  polymer  chain,  independent  of  the  molecular weight of the polymer but specific to each particular system polymer-solvent and depending on the mea/hyphenminus surement temperature. Within the NMR method, sys/hyphenminus tems of nuclear spins characterized by a single value of the  studied  parameter  are  usually  selected  as  phases [23]. The phases thus defined have nothing common with the thermodynamic phases of the state of a sub/hyphenminus stance.  Polymer  solutions  are  polyphase  systems, because they are characterized by a series of D values. The translation mobility of a polyphase polymer mac/hyphenminus romolecule as whole is often analyzed using the aver/hyphenminus age self/hyphenminusdiffusion coefficient ( D s ) [23, 25]:

$$D _ { s } = \Sigma p _ { i } D _ { i } ,$$
