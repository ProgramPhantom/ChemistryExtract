## Self-di ff usion of water and poly(amidoamine) dendrimers in dilute aqueous solutions †

Istv´ an B´ anyai,* a M´ onika K´ eri, a Zolt´ an Nagy, a M´ arta Berka a and Lajos P. Balogh b

Medium generation PAMAM dendrimers are extensively researched as drug delivery vehicles therefore detailed knowledge of their physico-chemical properties in solution is vital. We have selected ethylenediamine core, generation fi ve poly(amidoamine) (PAMAM\_E5) dendrimers (amine terminated PAMAM\_E5.NH2 and its succinamic acid derivative PAMAM\_E5.SAH, respectively) as model compounds to study their dynamic behavior in water as a function of pH and concentration. Di ff usion coe ffi cients of water and the dendrimers were determined in deuterium oxide using pulse- fi eld gradient stimulated echo (PGSE) NMR. The di ff usion rate for PAMAM\_E5.NH2 increased monotonously with increasing pH values while for PAMAM\_E5.SAH a maximum was found at an isoelectric value of pH ¼ 5.7. The apparent di ff usion coe ffi cients of dendrimers decreased linearly with increasing concentration measured at their self-pH (pH ¼ 9.4 for PAMAM\_E5.NH2 and pH ¼ 5.7 for PAMAM\_E5.SAH) in the absence of added salts. The observations could be explained by considering hard sphere interactions between strongly hydrated dendrimer molecules. The average hydrodynamic radii of dendrimers were determined by extrapolating the measured di ff usion coe ffi cients to zero dendrimer concentration and applying the Stokes -Einstein equation. The calculated values were R H ¼ 3.05  0.04 nm for PAMAM\_E5.NH2 and R H ¼ 3.37  0.08 nm for PAMAM\_E5.SAH respectively. Measured di ff usion coe ffi cients of water (D2O) also decreased linearly with increasing dendrimer concentration. From the concentration dependence, the average number of water molecules that form one dynamic unit with one macromolecule could be calculated on the basis of three di ff erent obstruction models. It has been concluded that hydrated PAMAM dendrimers in aqueous solutions behave as soft colloids against solvent molecules but as hard-sphere colloids against each other. Their equivalent hard-sphere radii were found to be equal to the measured hydrodynamic radii.

Low generation dendrimer molecules (LGD) are relatively small molecules or low molecular mass polymers (oligomers) with fully penetrable networks and a few reactive sites that are all equally accessible for solvent molecules (the termini are all on the ' surface ' of the dendrimer molecules). 2 High generation dendrimer molecules (HGD) are spherical organic nanoparticles with high structural symmetry and high relative density. In these dense organic nanoparticles, the kinetic accessibility of groups on the surface is much greater than that of groups in the interior space. Medium generation dendrimer molecules (MGD) are between LGD and HGD and display characteristics of both groups. These dendrimers are described to behave as hybrids between polymer chains and compact colloidal particles based on extensive theoretical calculations and experimental studies. 8,10,23 It is well known that the charge, inside structure and size of polyionic dendrimers change as a function of pH or concentration. All these factors a ff ect the di ff usivity of these macromolecules; therefore studying their di ff usion behavior in solution can be a unique source of information beyond the almost exclusively used small angle scattering methods. 23 Pulse- eld gradient stimulated echo (PGSE) NMR spectroscopy in combination with other NMR methods for solution structure and dynamics is an excellent method of choice to obtain data for solution behaviors of macromolecules in general and for those of polyionic dendrimer model systems as well. 24 -27

Soon a  er the  rst synthesis of arborol dendrimers (alkane core) the in  uence of pH on their size was observed. 11 It was reported that the size of third generation amine-terminated arborol dendrimers (determined from di ff usion coe ffi cients by the Einstein -Stokes equation) increased with decreasing pH. 28 This ' swelling ' was explained by the Coulomb repulsion between the protonated primary amine termini. Arborol dendrimers with carboxylic acid terminal groups behaved the opposite way, as deprotonation of terminal carboxylic groups caused an increase in size with decreasing pH. A small decrease was observed in basic solution with increasing pH but no explanation was provided. 11,28 In the case of PPI (poly-propyleneimine) dendrimers with carboxylic acid termini a size minimum appeared as a function of pH. 29 The explanation was that these PPI dendrimers contained not only carboxyl termini, but proton acceptor tertiary amino groups in the bifurcation points. At pH ¼ 6, the formally neutral dendrimer exhibited a minimum size, while both at pH ¼ 2 (positively charged) and pH ¼ 11 (negatively charged) larger hydrodynamic radii were observed. Increasing hydration with the increase of charge was suggested to explain the larger size of dendrimers at low and at high pH, although the electrostatic repulsion of charged atoms was also considered as an alternative. 29

Goddard and his co-workers reported extensive molecular dynamic calculations predicting variation in the size of EDA (ethylenediamine) core PAMAM\_E x .NH2 dendrimers as a function of pH. 14,15 They have calculated that the size of amine terminated PAMAM dendrimers increases with decreasing pH. Their results were interpreted as due to a signi  cant backfolding of ' surface ' amine groups into the interior of the macromolecules, even at low pH when both the tertiary and primary nitrogens are protonated. They found that the electrostatic repulsion between charged terminal -NH3 + groups caused only a small increase in size around neutral pH on the model used. At low pH, when the tertiary nitrogens were fully protonated, a larger increase in size was calculated. Penetration of water molecules into the interior of dendrimers was found to be an important factor in swelling of the molecules. 15 Most recently re  ned atomistic MD simulations showed that the radius of gyration of fourth generation PAMAM\_E4.NH2 dendrimer molecules was essentially pH independent in accordance with earlier experiments but the molecules underwent pH responsive conformational changes. 30,31

Lin et al. performed molecular dynamic simulations on properties of water molecules inside the PAMAM dendrimers. The results were interpreted in terms of three types of solvent molecules such as ' buried ' , ' surface ' and ' bulk ' ones, characterized by their number, dynamic behavior and by their spatial position in or around the dendrimer molecules. 13

The change of the apparent di ff usion coe ffi cient as a function of total concentration also provided useful information about the size and the nature of interactions between dendrimer molecules although the interpretation of data was not straightforward. 32,33 In dilute methanol solution (volume fraction less than 0.2) the di ff usion rate of PPI dendrimers from 1 to 5 generations decreased linearly with the increase of the total concentration. An e ff ective hard sphere radius could be determined for PPI dendrimers, which was larger than the radius of gyration but smaller than the hydrodynamic radius. From these results Rietveld and Bedeaux concluded that in dilute methanol solution no ' so  colloid ' interaction exists between dendrimer molecules. 32 The scaling behavior and hydrodynamic radii of G0 -G3 (E0 -E3) PAMAM.NH2 dendrimers were determined by PGSE NMR in water from the concentration dependence. 34

In order to gain deeper insight into the interaction, size variation and hydration of PAMAM dendrimers as a function of pH and total concentration in aqueous solution, we performed extended PGSE NMR studies to determine the di ff usion coe ffi cients for both dendrimers and water molecules in the same solution. We have chosen two EDA core, generation  ve PAMAM dendrimers with characteristically di ff erent termini: an amine terminated compound (PAMAM\_E5.NH2), and a succinamic acid derivative (PAMAM\_E5.SAH) (Scheme 1). Our goal was to study the application of colloid suspension theories for dilute aqueous solutions of hydrated PAMAM dendrimers of medium generation. 23 We expected a characteristic pH dependence of the di ff usion coe ffi cient of PAMAM\_E5.SAH because between pH ¼ 2 -8 this dendrimer changes its net charge from positive to negative as a function of pH. To determine the hydration of dendrimer molecules experimentally we implemented the method described for surfactants and colloidal systems. 35,36 To our knowledge this is the  rst report of experimental hydration data for PAMAM dendrimers; only a rough estimation was published before. 37

## Experimental

## Materials

Ethylenediamine (EDA) core PAMAM dendrimers of generation 5 (PAMAM\_E5.NH2, 1205\_06-E5.0-LD for short E5.NH2) were purchased from Dendritech (Midland, MI, USA) in methanol solution containing 14.17% E5.NH2. The methanol was removed using a rotavap, and the resulting solid was dissolved in water, dialyzed against distilled water using a MWCT ¼ 10 000 membrane (Pierce Scienti  c). This E5.NH2 dendrimer material contained 89.64% E5.NH2 as the major fraction, 8.11% (E5.NH2)2 dimers, and 2.25% of E4.NH2 dendrimers as the trailing generation. Its average molecular mass was equal to M w, (SEC) ¼ 30 720, M w/ M n ¼ 1.05. The PAMAM\_E5.SAH (for short E5.SAH) was synthesized and characterized as it is written in our previous papers. 38 -40 The measured data of molecular weight and polydispersity index: M w (SEC) ¼ 40 710, M w/ M n ¼ 1.045. KCl, NaOH, KOH, HCl and deuterium-oxide were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used without further treatments.

## Sample preparation

For di ff usion studies, solutions of di ff erent total concentrations of dendrimers were prepared between c ¼ 0.2 -1.1 mmol kg  1 for E5.SAH and c ¼ 0.4 -1.5 mmol kg  1 for E5.NH2 in D2O. The weighed amount of lyophilized dendrimers was dissolved in D2O, and diluted with the weighed amount of D2O. No further concentration determination was performed. Relative concentrations were checked by means of 1 H NMR integrals. For studying the pH dependence the lyophilized E5.NH2 dendrimer was dissolved in an appropriate amount of DCl solution. E5.SAH was dissolved in D2O which resulted in slightly acidic solution (pD y 5). The pH was set in both samples by using concentrated NaOH (in D2O) and concentrated HCl solutions to avoid substantial dilution. pD values were read and the pH was calculated as pH ¼ pD + 0.415. 41,42

## Instruments

The pH was measured using a Metrohm 877 pHlab pH meter using a Metrohm 6.0234 combined glass electrode. The pH potentiometric titration was performed with Metrohm 736 GP Titrino automatic titration systems in a volume of 4 cm 3 . 0.2 mol dm  3 KOH, standardized on KH-phthalate, was used for titration of acidi  ed dendrimer solutions. The ionic medium was constant, I ¼ 0.2 M KCl during the titration. The carbon dioxide was kept out by bubbling argon through the solution. The H + -ion concentration was determined from the measured pH by Irving's method. 42

1D and 2D, 1 H, and 13 C NMR experiments were performed usually at 300 K with Bruker DRX 400 and 360 MHz instruments using standard pulse programs provided by the manufacturer (see ESI † ) normal 5 mm direct QNP and inverse BB ( z gradient)

probe heads. The temperature was calibrated by the 1 H NMR chemical shi  of methanol. 43 This temperature could be kept constant by cooling air  ow of the lab temperature and electric heating controlled by an Eurotherm unit. We post-processed the spectra with Bruker WinNMR Ò so  ware.

For di ff usion measurements the pulse  eld gradient stimulated spin echo sequence (PGSE) was used with longitudinal eddy-current delay (LED) and bipolar gradient pulses (BIPLED) by the standard program provided with the spectrometer. 44 The di ff usion of dendrimer was measured on peaks of CH2 protons, while the water di ff usion (D2O) on HOD peak. Deuterium NMR would have been an option as well, but 1 H NMR is technically easier and provides us the same information. In di ff usion measurements, we used a constant di ff usion time ( D ), typically 10 -15 ms, and gradient pulse length ( d ), 4 ms, for water, while 50 -60 ms and 8 -10 ms for dendrimers. In general, 3 -5 parallel measurements were performed on one sample with di ff erent combinations of the above parameters. The pulsed gradient strength ( G ) increased with 16 square distant steps from 0 to approximately 50 Gauss cm  1 . We accepted the calculated di ff usion coe ffi cients when the values di ff ered less than 1% in the case of water and less than 3% in the case of dendrimers.

## Results and discussion

## Potentiometric data

Protonation constants and the average number of the functional groups of dendrimers were determined from the titration curves. For E5.NH2 a simple solution equilibrium model su ffi ciently describes the titration curves. In this model the primary amines and the tertiary nitrogen atoms are considered as independent protonation sites. The best  t of data results in an average 119 tertiary nitrogen atoms and 122.6 primary nitrogen atoms (formula values are 126 and 128 respectively). The two p K values are 5.72(2) and 8.90(2) in 0.2 mol dm  3 KCl, similar to those published by Cakara et al. 48,49 We performed the same  tting procedures for E5.SAH. The p K values are 4.60(1) and 6.69(1) corresponding to -COOH 117 and tertiary nitrogen 108. The p K of carboxylate termini are somewhat higher, while the p K of tertiary nitrogens are considerably lower than those published for carboxylated PPI dendrimers. 50 Although the literature describes other methods to analyze the titration curves (see ESI † ), 48,50,51 according to the sensitivity of our titration the donor groups seem to bind protons quite independently in both dendrimers and this simple approach is satisfactory for the qualitative interpretation of di ff usion properties.

## High resolution NMR results

1 H NMR spectra of dendrimers are usually poor in signals as a consequence, the  ne structure of the 1 H NMR spectra caused by scalar couplings is not always observed as shown in Fig. 1. Thus assignment and structural analysis requires a lot of care and good technical background. 2D COSY spectra were used to assign scalar coupling protons, being present on neighboring carbons. 52,53 13 C NMR spectra through HSQC and HMBC were used for complete assignment. The reason for the poor resolution is that the protons in identical structural positions but in di ff erent shells ( e.g. 8, 12, 16 and 20) are not perfectly the same; slight di ff erences in chemical shi  s may occur. The relaxation broadening of the peaks is o  en large enough to mask completely the  ne structure of the spectrum.

The reasons for the decrease of relative di ff usion rate with increasing concentration are the hydrodynamic interactions resulting in an increase of the viscosity of the suspension, direct interaction and the Brownian force. 57 Fig. 4 and Table 2 show that E5.SAH behaves similarly. Our results con  rm those ones described for di ff usion of the   h generation poly(propylene imine) dendrimers in methanol. 32 However in that case the ' e ff ective hard sphere radius ' was between the hydrodynamic radius and the radius of gyration. In our case both hydrodynamic radii are the ' e ff ective hard sphere radius ' . The possible explanation is that PAMAM molecules are better solvated in water than PPI in methanol.

When the ' hard sphere radius ' of E5.NH2 (determined by SANS 58 in dilute aqueous solution) is used to calculate the volume fraction ( f HS) the negative slope is larger,  3.5 for E5.NH2 (Fig. 2 and Table 2). If this calculation was correct this steeper decrease can be explained by additional interactions between the dendrimer molecules. ' So  ' or ' ultraso  ' interactions were introduced to describe this behavior by Likos and Ballauf et al. 8,10,23,61 They have suggested that PAMAM dendrimers exhibit an e ff ective interaction potential that may be described by a Gaussian function. 62,63 However, this type of interaction probably exists in more concentrated solution of less solvating medium, than that applied in the present work. This interaction was observed experimentally for PPI dendrimers in methanol and poly(allylcarbosilane) dendrimers in chloroform at higher concentrations. 32,64

If we used f M volume fractions (calculated from density values) the slopes would be  6.6 and  6.7 (Table 2). Such a steep decrease of di ff usion rate could only be explained by selfassociation. 65,66 Applying L´ evay's approximation for the linear dependence of D d on the molar concentration (shown in Fig. 2) the slope is equal to  2 K ( D 0 d  D dimer), where K is the equilibrium constant and D dimer is the di ff usion constant of noncovalent dimeric association while D 0 d is the intercept. The D dimer may be estimated as D 0/1.38. 67 From the concentration dependence shown in Fig. 2 K can be numerically estimated as 120 (the slope is 1.1 -10  8 ) for E5.NH2 and 160 (slope is 1.4 -10  8 ) for E5.SAH. These ' dimerization ' constants would mean that under our experimental conditions 20 -30% of dendrimers were in dimeric associate form, which is improbable. Our conclusion is that the hydrated generation 5 PAMAM dendrimers behave like hard sphere colloids in dilute aqueous solutions. The effective hard sphere radius is equal to the hydrodynamic radius. The probable reason is the strong hydration which competes efficiently with the expected so  or ultraso  interactions .

The di ff usion rates of dendrimers are pH dependent but the di ff usion rate of water is insensitive to pH (Fig. 5). In the case of E5.NH2, the increase of D d with increasing pH leads to saturation a  er pH ¼ 9. Earlier MD calculations indicated that the deprotonation of tertiary nitrogens caused a larger decrease in size than the deprotonation of terminal -NH3 + groups. 15 Later, re  nements with di ff erent force  elds resulted in smaller pH sensitivities. 31 Although our di ff usion coe ffi cients may be related to the change of hydrodynamic radius with pH, we cannot calculate R H here from D values due to the lack of viscosity data. (The adjustment of pH was managed by adding HCl (vapor of conc . HCl solution) and concentrated NaOD solution without the control of the salt concentration.) The pH dependence should be studied in ionic medium, but in that case the role of the electric double layer must be considered. The data show in Fig. 5 that the hydrodynamic size is pH sensitive and the deprotonation causes a decrease in the size. From these experiments we cannot distinguish the role of tertiary and primary nitrogen in changing the size. For E5.SAH there is a maximum in the observed di ff usion constant at isoelectric pH (PZC) indicating the smallest size. This is similar to what Rietveld et al. found for PPI-carboxylate dendrimers in water measured at three pH values. 29 For E5.SAH, the measured di ff usion coe ffi cients have much less uncertainty than that for E5.NH2, because the self-pH is the PZC; therefore a much less amount of electrolyte had to be added to regulate the pH. Since the pH-dependent structure and dynamics of PAMAM dendrimers are not available the detailed experiments are in progress in our laboratory.

## Di ff usion of water

In Fig. 6, the decrease in the relative di ff usion rate of water ( D obs w / D 0 w( f ¼ 0)) with increasing concentration of dendrimers is shown. ## Conclusions

We report here the experimental concentration dependence of the apparent self-di ff usion coe ffi cient ( D d) of the PAMA-M\_E5.NH2 dendrimer and its succinamic derivative (PAMA-M\_E5.SAH) as determined by PGSE NMR in dilute solutions (0.1 -2 mM). From the extrapolation of di ff usion coe ffi cient to zero concentration the hydrodynamic radii, R H, of E5.NH2 and E5.SAH were found to be 3.05 nm and 3.37 nm respectively. From the slope of the volume fraction dependence of the apparent di ff usion coe ffi cient we concluded that generation 5 hydrated PAMAM dendrimers behave like hard sphere colloids with an ' e ff ective hard sphere ' radius equal to their R H in dilute aqueous solutions.

Approximately 3600 -3700 water molecules are under the in  uence of one dendrimer molecule if no obstruction e ff ect is considered. This number is higher than that predicted from MD calculations or deduced from SANS experiments for E5.NH2. We explain the di ff erences as follows: MD calculations consider only water molecules that ful  ll narrowly prede  ned conditions, i.e. the pre-given distance from the ' surface ' of the dendrimer, positions inside or outside of the dendrimer; from SANS experiments those water molecules can only be calculated that occupy the ' inside cavities ' of the dendrimer molecules, therefore SANS does not deal with the ' slow ' water molecules around E5.NH2. However, the PGSE NMR method can detect water molecules bound weakly to terminal NH2 groups of PAMAM\_E5.NH2. In the case of PAMAM\_E5.SAH the number of water molecules determined corresponds exactly to the di ff erence between the hydrodynamic volume and the self-volume of the dendrimer if we use the ' no obstruction ' model. However if we take into account the obstruction of the organic skeleton we had to suggest the existence of non-exchanging water molecules of structural origin. Both models have advantages but the theoretical basis of the ' no obstruction ' model is weaker. From these results we can conclude that PGSE NMR, supported by potentiometry and high resolution NMR, appears to be well suited to study and describe the hydration and dynamic behavior of dissolved dendritic macromolecules in water and opens a new route for understanding the hydration dynamics of so  colloids.
