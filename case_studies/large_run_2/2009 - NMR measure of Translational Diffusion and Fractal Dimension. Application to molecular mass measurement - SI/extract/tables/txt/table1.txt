For each data set, the spectral axis was processed with sine-bell, and Fourier transform was applied in order to obtain 1024 real points. A spline baseline correction was finally applied. The columns (axis of varying gradient) of this data-set were then processed for inverse Laplace transform using the Inverse Laplace technique [5] using the Gifa software [12]. The DOSY reconstruction was realized with 490 points in the diffusion dimension and 2000 MaxEnt iterations. For each protein, the diffusion coefficient was calculated as the mean value of the diffusion coefficients observed for the 15 most intense columns in the 0.22 - 2.46 ppm region. The uncertainty on the measure was estimated from the standard deviation σ on these values, and chosen at ± σ (95% confidence level). For each data set, the diffusion coefficient of the Tris molecule was calculated from the column at 3.70 ppm. The results are summarized in 1.

## A.2. Polymer study

Polymers were measured either on a 600 MHz (PS in toluene) or on a 400 MHz spectrometer (other studies). Polyethyleneoxide (PEO) and polystyrene (PS) calibrated monodisperse polymers were purchased from Polymer Labs or Amerian Polymer Standards Corporation, the reported polydispersity index being typically between 1.02 and 1.10. Materials were used as received. PEO standards were stored in a refrigerator to minimize degradation. Measures were performed at 30 ◦ C. The diffusion time was typi- cally 50 ms, increased to a range of 100 to 350 ms for the analysis of ultra-high molecular mass polymers, such as the 7,800 and 18,000 kDa PS samples. The number of scan was adapted to the sample, but never less than eight scans. The total experiment time, including a 4 min temperature equilibration step, ranged from 15 minutes to several hours per sample All experiment (except for PS in toluene) were acquired as follows. The 1 H PFG NMR diffusion experiments utilized a Bruker Avance-400 spectrometer operating at a 1 H frequency of 400.1 MHz, equipped with a 5 mm multinuclear broadband-observe z-axis gradient probe capable of generating 55 G/cm field strengths. The PFG NMR experiments employed a convection-compensating double stimulated-echo pulse sequence [8] with three 0.6-ms sinusoidal-shaped spoiler gradient pulses. Smoothed-square gradient pulses with durations typically in the range of 4-14 ms were used for each of the four diffusion pulses. eight spectra were obtained using varying gradient strengths.
