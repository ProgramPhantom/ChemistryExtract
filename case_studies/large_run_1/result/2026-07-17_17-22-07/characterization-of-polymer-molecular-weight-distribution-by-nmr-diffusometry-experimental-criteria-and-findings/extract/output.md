ABSTRACT: NMR di ff usometry fi nds useful applications in characterizing molecular weight ( M ) and molecular weight distribution (MWD) for polymers due to its unique advantages in generic detection, chemical selectivity, and quantitation. We use the critical dilute concentration C dilute * to explicitly delineate the boundary of the su ffi ciently dilute condition, below which chain interactions have a negligible impact on polymer di ff usion. We present solid evidence to validate the postulated theory that links C dilute * to molecular weight, polydispersity, and chain conformation. ## ■ INTRODUCTION

Polymers undoubtedly represent one of the most important classes of functional materials that play an indispensable role in modern industry and daily life. Analysis of molecular weight ( M ) and molecular weight distribution (MWD) is crucial to direct polymer synthesis and processing since this information fundamentally impacts the properties and performance of polymeric materials in many aspects, ranging from physical properties to mechanical properties to chemical resistance. 1 -3 Though gel permeation chromatography (GPC) is a wellknown technology suited for M /MWD characterization, NMR di ff usometry continues to fi nd expanding applications in M / MWD characterization 4 -6 due to its uniquely combined advantages in generic detection, chemical selectivity, nondestructiveness, and quantitative nature. The technique 7 utilizes one or several pairs of transient magnetic fi eld gradient to couple the detected NMR signal with the average molecular displacement, allowing determination of the di ff usion coe ffi cient associated with individual NMR peaks to quantify M / MWDmeanwhile preserving the spectroscopic resolution. This renders NMR some superiority to other techniques, such as GPC and mass spectrometer (MS) in polymer characterization, which often are lacking some of these important features. Easy sample preparation, fl exible choice of solvent, and a broad range of operating temperature further strengthen the practicality of di ff usion measurement on a routine basis. As research and development progresses, these advantages of NMR di ff usometry have become overwhelmingly important when studying polymer systems with increased structural complexity, 5,6,8,9 which often necessitates more detailed characterization. Under the dilute condition, polymer di ff usion ( D ) is related to M based on the following equation 4

$$\int _ { \mathbb { N } } ^ { 0 } \ D = k M ^ { - \alpha }$$

α is the Mark -Houwink coe ffi cient and k is a constant that depends on the polymer system. By utilizing a set of monodisperse polymer standards to determine k and α to establish the correlation between D and M , 4,10 generic determination of the apparent M /MWD of unknown polymeric materials becomes viable.

The past decades have witnessed numerous research e ff orts using NMR di ff usion to investigate polymer systems from di ff erent aspects, including chain conformations, 11 -13 polymer size, 11 M and MWD of polymers and peptides, 5,9,10,14 -17 and the development of new methods to determine M / MWD. 4,15,18 -22 However, close examination reveals that data disparity often exists when comparing literature results. Chen et al. reported a value of k = 2.40 × 10 -8 (e.g., 10 -7.62 ) and α = 0.62 using standards with M in the range from 1 to 500k. In contrast, Håkansson et al. reported the value of k = 1.0 × 10 -8 and α = 0.55 using standards with M in the range from ∼ 1 to 900k. Though both groups claimed that the measurement was performed under su ffi ciently dilute conditions, where polymer chain interactions are negligible, the drastic di ff erence between their results undoubtedly signi fi es the presence of experimental inconsistency. Interestingly, such discrepancy is hardly mentioned or discussed in the literature. It also leaves a question mark regarding what values of k and α (for PEO) should be used for reliable analysis of M /MWD. In another example, Arrabal-Campos et al. and Li et al. have both conducted NMR di ff usion on monodisperse polystyrene (PS) in benzene at 21 and 25 ° C, respectively. 14,15 However, their results showed a signi fi cant di ff erence in the α value (0.568 vs 0.537) for the same system, which cannot be simply explained by the minor di ff erence in temperature. Di ff usion of PS in chloroform has also been extensively studied by NMR and used as a standard for M /MWD analysis, yet a wide range of α values have been reported. 5,12,21,22 These data disparities, if not treated with care, will translate into undesired systematic deviations in the determined M /MWD and it further brings up some fundamental questions: what causes the disparity and how to resolve the problem?

Unlike small molecules, polymer chains tend to swell in a good solvent and interact with each other to restrict chain motion. Such interactions become further exacerbated as concentration increases and consequently lead to a reduction in polymer di ff usion ( D polymer ). It was noted in both Chen and Håkansson ' s work that D polymer should be measured under the ' su ffi ciently ' dilute condition where polymer chain interactions have a negligible impact. Otherwise, the determined M /MWD will be in fl ated due to the reduced D polymer . Though the critical overlap concentration C * is widely used to characterize the dilute condition, 23 it is often not clear how far below C * can be reliably treated as su ffi ciently dilute in the context of D polymer measurement. In fact, a common problem associated with M / MWD characterization using D polymer stems from the selection of the aforementioned su ffi ciently dilute condition. A seemingly low concentration, typically between 0.5 and 4.5 mg/mL, has been used in the majority of polymer di ff usion studies for M /MWD characterization, 4 -6,8 -12,14 -22 yet solid justi fi cation is rarely provided. In some extreme cases, the polymer concentration is even up to ∼ 80 mg/mL. 13 From the perspective of a general audience, a more practical question arises: what is the reliability to leverage the literature results for M /MWD characterization?

In this report, we present a fundamental study on D polymer in a solution with the aim to address the above questions. Our goal is twofold: establish a standard protocol to ensure consistent and reliable M /MWD characterization by NMR di ff usion and present a new analytical capability that allows characterizing polymer architecture and globular structure through di ff usion analysis. To facilitate illustration, the rest of the paper will be organized as follows: fi rst, we introduce the concept of critical dilute concentration C dilute * to de fi ne the su ffi ciently dilute condition for D polymer measurement. We then present a theory based on the scaling law to show how C dilute * is connected to relevant structural characteristics of polymer chains. Second, we design and conduct robust di ff usion measurement to validate the postulated theory that captures new insights regarding polymer chain interactions in a solution. Finally, we demonstrate the importance of our validated theory in applications of polymer characterization and will brie fl y discuss the impact on M /MWD analysis if D polymer is not measured under su ffi ciently dilute condition. These fi ndings not only establish guidance for M /MWD characterization by NMR di ff usion but also enable technical advancement, particularly in studying polymer architecture and globular structure using NMR spectroscopy and di ff usometry.

For GPC analysis, the sample was prepared in a HLPC grade tetrahydrofuran at a concentration of 2 mg/mL and allowed to dissolve for 2 h on a fl atbed shaker. The standards were run on a Waters Model e2695 at a nominal fl ow rate of 1.0 mL/min of HPLC grade tetrahydrofuran. For NMR analysis, PMMA and PS samples were dissolved in deuterated chloroform (CDCl3). PEO and egg white protein samples were dissolved in deuterated water (D 2 O). Typically, ∼ 10 -15 mg of sample was accurately weighed on a balance (accurate to 0.1 mg) to prepare a concentrated stock solution, e.g., 1 wt %. An aliquot of the stock solution was then transferred and weighed into a glass vial and further diluted to the target concentration by adding a speci fi c amount of solvent with 1 mM chromium(III) acetylacetonate (as the relaxation agent). The dilution level was further cross checked spectroscopically for consistency. For PMMA, 825 ± 5 mg of the above-diluted solution was consistently added into a 5 mm NMR tube. Similarly, 590 ± 10 mg of each PEO or protein sample solution was consistently added into a 5 mm NMR tube. All of the samples were freshly prepared and equilibrated at 25 ° C for 10 min prior to any di ff usion measurement. Solvent evaporation was negligible for most of the experiments (&lt;1 h) and the NMR tube was sealed when experimental time lasted longer than 5 h. 1 H NMR di ff usion measurements were conducted on a 400 MHz Bruker Avance spectrometer equipped with a broad band 5 mm probe. The probe temperature was calibrated and controlled at 25 ± 0.1 ° C. Gradient calibration was performed by measuring di ff usion of residual 1 H2O in D2O to yield a self-di ff usion coe ffi cient of 1.90 × 10 -9 m 2 /s. 26 Pulsed gradient double spin echo (PGDSE) 27 was employed to measure PMMA and PEO di ff usion successively by monitoring the variation of the I -OCH3 and I -OCH2 signal as a function of magnetic fi eld gradient g . Pulsed gradient double stimulated echo (PGDSTE) 28 was employed to measure PS and protein di ff usion successively. Around 4 -4800 scans were recorded to generate adequate spectra sensitivity with a total repetition time being 4 -5 × of the longitudinal relaxation time T 1 . ## ■ RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Evaluation of eq 4 requires reliable determination of C dilute * , which necessitates high-quality di ff usion data. We design the experiment by selecting monodisperse poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA) as a surrogate to polystyrene (PS) standards due to its similarity to PS in many aspects 29 and the lack of homonuclear coupling interference on the -OCH3 group along the polymer backbone. This allowed utilizing the double spin echo 27 for di ff usion measurement to e ff ectively suppress thermal convection without compromising the spectra sensitivity. In theory, spectra sensitivity using double spin echo is 4 × higher than a double stimulated echo. Due to the relaxation di ff erence between longitudinal relaxation T 1 and transverse relaxation T 2 , ∼ 3.3 × of sensitivity gain is achieved for PMMA using double spin echo as compared to the double stimulated echo, 28 which leads to a signi fi cant reduction of experimental time ( ∼ 10 × ) and it represents a crucial step for e ffi cient and reliable analysis. Figure 1a representatively shows di ff usion spectra of PMMA in deuterated chloroform (CDCl 3 ) vs variation of gradient strength, g . The characteristic -OCH3 peak of PMMA and solvent peaks are labeled accordingly. The measured D PMMA is free of artifact, which is con fi rmed by its independence on di ff usion time Δ and the excellent fi t shown in Figure 1b,c, respectively. Chloroform di ff usion ( D CHCl3 ) is measured separately using lower gradient strengths. For PMMA, spectra quality is characterized by the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of -OCH3 peak in the di ff usion spectrum when g ∼ 0. For a given SNR, the relative standard deviation σ RSD (SI, eq S11) is calculated by averaging 20 replicates, and the relationship between σ RSD and SNR is well characterized by the power law ( R 2 = 0.999), as shown in Figure 1d. The result is consistently leveraged to all D PMMA measurements with SNR ∼ 350 being selected ( σ RSD &lt; 0.25%) as the experimental criteria so that high-precision D PMMA can be measured down to the concentration level ∼ 40 ppm in 3.5 h.

Taking one of the examples mentioned in the introduction, the di ff erence in α values reported by Arrabal-Campos et al. and Li et al. for the same system can be just rationalized based on the above analysis. We have demonstrated the critical role of C dilute * to specify the su ffi ciently dilute condition for M and MWD characterization and further linked it to chain conformation, M and PDI. Polymer and solvent di ff usion respond di ff erently to concentration variation, highlighting the unique role of chain interactions even at concentrations signi fi cantly below C * . Quantitative analysis indicates that D polymer measurement above the su ffi ciently dilute condition would lead to a systematic and potentially large error in M and MWD analysis. These fi ndings help to understand the data disparity in the literature and provide useful guidance toward M /MWD characterization by NMR di ff usion measurements. The study o ff ers key insights that lead to the discovery of the invariant macromolecular di ff usion on concentration for globular structure and provides a di ff erent approach for characterizing polymer molecular weight and chain architecture.
