After having established the dependence of D on M w and polymer concentration, it was necessary to verify the correctness of this relationship before performing the ' One NMR Tube Experiment ' . Therefore, a kinetic experiment on L-lactide polymerization was performed with sampling of the polymerization mixture ( protocol and conditions of experiments are given in ESI † ). The determined molar masses are given in Table 2.

The data in Table 2 (as well as Fig. S8 in ESI † ) show, that for the dead macromolecules, both the DOSY and SEC methods give almost identical values of M n and M w. This observation additionally indicates that the relationship between M w and D has been correctly determined. Moreover, Đ of polymer samples range from 1.00 to 1.07, as expected for chains formed in the controlled-living polymerization.

The monomer conversion and degree of polymerization P n were determined from integrations of the standard 1 H NMR spectrum (Fig. 3). The spectrum shows the structure of active macromolecules being in equilibrium with the temporarily inactive chains with -OH end groups, in agreement with ref. 15. The monomer conversion was calculated from the ratio of the integration of the signal from methine protons of the lactoyl units e (overlapped quartets at δ = 5.0 -5.1 ppm) to the integration of the monomer signal (quartet centred at δ = 3.75 ppm). Signals e ′ and g broadened due to an equilibrium between active and inactive chains and therefore cannot be used for calculations of P n of living macromolecules. P n and M n were calculated from the ratio of the integrations of signals e and d ′ (the latter at δ = 4.89 ppm).
