ABSTRACT: It is well-known that the molar mass distribution (MMD) of block copolymers will be performed by size exclusion chromatography (SEC) with two calibrated detectors to determine the molar mass distribution via the average chemical composition distribution (CCD) of the block copolymers. This work will present a new way to determine the molar mass distribution via the average chemical composition of block copolymers by using the nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) based diffusion ordered spectroscopy (DOSY) with inverse Laplace transform (ILT). This DOSY approach allows the determination of the MMD of Supporting Information

copolymers in the bulk sample. It will be shown that DOSY can be enabled for the determination of the MMD via the CCD of block copolymers by adopting the dual detector SEC approach to DOSY. Therefore, several PSb -PMMA block copolymers were analyzed by this new DOSY approach as well as the dual SEC-UV-RI detection. The DOSY results of the MMD and CCD are in good agreement with the common SEC-RI-UV dual detector copolymer analysis.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Block copolymers have a wide range of applications like drug delivery, 1 3D printing, 2 and dispersion. 3 The study of copolymers is very important because the chemical composition and chain length strongly influence the physical and chemical properties of these polymers.

Whereas 1 H NMR is typically used to determine the average chemical composition of block copolymers and to some extent the number-average molar mass M n via the end group of the polymer, DOSY enables the separation of mixtures, allows for the determination of the hydrodynamic radius, and also for obtaining the weight-average molar mass M w. The relation between the diffusion coefficient D and the weight-average molar mass M w of the polymer is given by the scaling equation as follows. 4

$$D = a M _ { w } ^ { b } & & ( 1 ) & \quad \text {tivels} \\$$

The parameter b describes the solvent quality, with the Flory exponent b ∼ -0.6 for a good solvent.

In the case of homopolymers even the determination of the molar mass distribution could be performed with DOSY including the calculation of the weight-average molar mass M w and number-average molar mass M n , and PDI. 5 -16 Most works deal with M w of polymers determined due to the relating calibration of the diffusion coefficient according to eq 1. 3.2. Copolymer Composition with DOSY. To determine the molar mass distributions of block copolymers with DOSY, we want to adopt the same approach of the SEC-UV-RI dual detector analysis to the DOSY studies. Therefore, four PSb -PMMA copolymer solutions of different compositions and chain lengths were prepared for the copolymer analysis (Table 1). PSb -PMMA copolymers were chosen because of the possibility to determine diffusion coefficients and signal intensities for each copolymer block separately by analyzing separated signals. The aromatic protons were chosen for the PS block, and the methoxy group was used for the PMMA block.

Self-diffusion coefficients of copolymers were determined by varying the gradient strength in 128 linear steps between 3 and 98% of the maximum gradient strength. The gradient strength and diffusion delays were chosen such as that the signal intensity of the methoxy group at 3.6 ppm was &lt;1% for the strongest gradient (98%) compared to the signal intensity at 3% gradient strength. According to these preparation conditions, the 2D-DOSY experiments were performed. The results are illustrated in Figure 2 showing the PMMA methoxy group and the aromatic protons of PS.

Figure 2. 2D-DOSY plot of PSb -PMMA pp84k block copolymer showing the methoxy group (3.6 ppm) of PMMA and the aromatic protons (7.3 -6.4 ppm) of PS.

<!-- image -->

The 2D-DOSY plot presents separated signals for each block. This is an ideal condition for the DOSY-ILT analysis as both signals of the aromatics and the methoxy group can be analyzed independently. After proton signal integrations of these two species at each gradient strength, the processing including the inverse Laplace transform was performed by using Bruker's Dynamics Center (Experimental Part 2.4). The obtained ILT intensities of the aromatics and methoxy group for each respective diffusion coefficient were then used to determine the weight-average chemical compositions at each diffusion coefficient.

The weight chemical compositions are the basic assumption for the calculation of the molar mass distribution of block copolymers as seen below. In the case of the SEC-UV-RI dual detector analysis, the weight chemical compositions are calculated by using so-called response factors of the two detectors. The measured response factors of the UV and RI detectors are shown in Table S5. The equations and experimental data for determining these factors are given in Table S7 and Figure S10. The UV and RI chromatograms of the copolymers are shown in Figure S11.

The results of all investigated PSb -PMMA by the DOSYILT block copolymer analysis compared to the SEC-UV-RI dual detector analysis are illustrated in Figure 3.

The comparison shows the same behavior for the chemical compositions for both methods even considering the lower DOSY-ILT resolution. The diffusion experiments were acquired at 128 gradient strengths. The ILT was performed for each PSb -PMMA copolymer and yields about 20 data points per copolymer compared to ∼ 100 data points by using the SEC-UV-RI dual detector analysis. The copolymers pp50k, pp81k, and pp84k polymers also contain remaining PS homopolymer. This is nicely indicated for both the DOSYILT and the SEC-UV-RI analysis. Looking at the diffusion data, the PS intensity curve (red) is broader than the PMMA intensity curve (blue) and is also ending at larger diffusion coefficients than the PMMA curve. This can only be caused by a second component within the lower molar mass range of M w besides the main copolymer molar masses. A pure block copolymer would lead to just one diffusion coefficient for both blocks, as these belong to the same macromolecule. These remaining homopolymer components lead to larger diffusion coefficients, but the data processing cannot separate both components because the differences of these molar masses are too small to completely determine the diffusion coefficient for each component. 33,34 The same could be observed for the SEC-UV-RI analysis where a little homopolymer component can be observed at larger retention times. These figures also demonstrate for the copolymers pp50k, pp81k, and pp84k that the RI and UV signal intensities are still detected for PS when the PMMA signal intensity almost dropped to zero. The aromatic system of polystyrene shows UV absorption at 254 nm. At the same wavelength, PMMA shows almost no absorption which is supported by the response factors as high values indicate strong absorption (Table S5). DOSY-ILT has one great advantage over the dual detector analysis as each block is represented by signals with a unique chemical shift, which can be analyzed independently. SEC dual detector analysis allows for investigating both blocks, but these detectors usually detect both homopolymers with different responses simultaneously. In most cases, there is no possibility to determine the blocks completely independent from each other with SEC.

Both analytical approaches revealed homopolymer contaminations for each PSb -PMMA copolymer, but DOSY-ILT can be used to determine a true or at least more accurate molar mass distribution. Therefore, the 1 H signal of the block that shows no homopolymer contamination is used to calculate M T n and M T w with the corresponding calibration that represents the larger block of the copolymer. As the diffusion coefficient distributions do not contain smaller homopolymer, which would increase the average diffusion coefficient, the new calculated molar mass distributions should indicate greater M n and M w values. The molar mass distribution values of the block which also presents the homopolymer contamination are illustrated in italic as these values do not represent the copolymer correctly.

All results are listed in Table 3 and compared to the SECUV-RI copolymer analysis data.

When SEC-UV-RI data are compared with the corresponding DOSY-ILT results, simply stated, they match. Some polymers show a better consistency than others, but the chemical compositions as discussed before as well as the molar mass distributions are agreeing very well. Considering for instance PSb -PMMA pp84k (42000/42000), it shows almost the same number- and weight-average molar masses as the SEC-UV-RI analysis. This polymer has the smallest contamination of PS homopolymer, which may be the reason for the almost identical molar mass distribution and matches the given average molar mass data of the manufacturer. The pp81k instead contains more PS homopolymer, which may lead to deviations of the manufacturer's M w distribution but shows the same results for the chromatographic and DOSY-ILT analysis. The pp50k and pp108k have the largest deviations from the stated weight distributions but still show similar results regarding DOSY-ILT and SEC-UV-RI block copolymer analysis. In addition, the chemical compositions can now also be shown in dependence of the molar masses. These data are shown in Figure S14 and demonstrate the same behavior for both analytical methods. Only small shifts of the chemical compositions versus molar mass are visible for the different copolymers. In general, it can be noted that the chemical compositions determined with DOSY-ILT reveal the same trend as the SEC-UV-RI analysis.

Furthermore, comparing the M p values of both analytical approaches, the M p data of the component without homopolymer contamination should be chosen over the other block as the remaining homopolymer shifts the diffusion coefficient to higher diffusion values.

The question remains which approach is the more accurate one. The traditional SEC has proven that it is a reliable and well-established analytical method. 35 -39 The DOSY-ILT approach instead shows accurate results compared to SEC dual detector analysis even if the inverse Laplace transform is just a mathematical interpretation of the acquired data. There is no need of choosing the right column, temperature, flow, and appropriate detector as the diffusion experiments are prepared fast and easy after the weighed sample is solved and filled into the NMR tube. The experiment time for a DOSY may be longer than the time for chromatographic separation, but even with 128 increments used, it was no longer than 3 h.

## 4. CONCLUSION

The DOSY-ILT block copolymer analysis approach shows excellent results as verified by the traditional SEC dual detector analysis and allows a fast and easy copolymer characterization. No second detector is needed as proton diffusion NMR experiments deliver separated chemical shifts for the PS and the PMMA block. The determined chemical compositions as well as molar mass distributions are comparable to the results of the traditional chromatographic approach, even if the resolution is lower. The main advantage of using DOSY-ILT over SEC-dual detector analysis is the determination of the true molar mass distribution as the block, which does not contain homopolymer contamination, reflects the true diffusion coefficient of the block copolymer.
