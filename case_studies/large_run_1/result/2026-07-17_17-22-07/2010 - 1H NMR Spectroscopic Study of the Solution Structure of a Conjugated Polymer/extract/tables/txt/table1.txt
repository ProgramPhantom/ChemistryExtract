In CDCl3 the signals from alkoxy protons C and D appear very close to each other, whereas in the aromatic solvents the signals shift to lower frequencies relative to their positions in CDCl3 and they are dispersed in the dimension of /c100 as a result of the interaction between these protons and the /c112 -electron field of the aromatic solvent. This is consistent with the well known phenomenon of aromatic solvent induced shift (ASIS) of protons attached to polar groups. 13 The observed low frequency shift of the signals indicates a non-specific interaction-geometry between the alkoxy side-groups and the aromatic solvent. This is because the area of the shielding zone of the solvent (normal to the plane of the aromatic ring) is somewhat larger relative to the area of the deshielding zone (along the edges of the aromatic ring). 14 On the contrary, however, the signals from the protons of the /c112 -conjugated polymer backbone (A and B) suffer higher frequency shifts in aromatic solvents relative to those in CDCl3 solution, which appear to suggest that the geometry of the interaction between the polymer backbone and the aromatic solvent favored deshielding of these protons. Now the /c112 -/c112 specific interaction in aromatic systems is known to favor an offset face-to-face stacked or an edge-to-face (T-shaped) geometry between the aromatic moieties. 15-17 However, X-ray diffraction studies on solvent-cast MEH-PPV films show that the phenylene plane of the polymer stack along the crystallographic a -axis of the unit cell. 18,19 Moreover, recent theoretical calculations show that the stacking geometry for /c112 -/c112 interaction becomes most significant for large unsaturated systems (&gt;1015 C atoms). 20 Hence segments of the hairy-rod type /c112 -conjugated polymers would fall in this category. From these studies it is quite reasonable to believe that inter-segment /c112 -/c112 stacking might be the natural mode of interaction in /c112 -conjugated polymer systems. The presence of such inter- action in the solution state would be manifested by a lowfrequency shift of the proton resonances of the stacked /c112 -conjugated moieties as they would mutually lie in the shielding zone produced by the aromatic ring current. 4,21 The appearance of the backbone proton resonances at much lower frequencies in CDCl3 relative to their positions in the aromatic solvents (by ca . 0.6 ppm in benzene-d6 for A, Table 1) indicates a stronger /c112 -/c112 self-stacking interaction of the polymer segments in the aliphatic solvent. The chemist's rule of thumb 'like dissolves like' predicts that an aliphatic solvent would solvate the 'hairy' aliphatic sidechains whereas an aromatic solvent would tend to solvate the conjugated backbone of the polymer. Thus a reasonable explanation for the above shift is that in CDCl3, an unfavorable interaction between the polymer backbone and the solvent leads the MEH-PPV segments to remain close to eachother forming stronger /c112 -/c112 complex through self-stacking. As aromatic solvents solvate the polymer backbone, they can compete for the /c112 -/c112 interaction with the polymer backbone and thus disrupt the polymer-polymer self-stacking tendency. Consequently the signals from the protons of the backbone /c112 -system appear at higher frequencies in aromatic solvents relative to those in CDCl3. 4 As will be evident from Fig. 1, this disrupting ability increases in the order - chlorobenzene &lt; toluene &lt; benzene. In this context, we should mention of a literature report that suggests strong coupling between the phenyl ring of the MEH-PPV backbone and the aromatic solvent (toluene-d8 and pyridine-d5) through polymer-solvent /c112 -/c112 stacking while the vinylic protons remain unaffected. 22 This causes, according to them, deshielding of the phenylic protons and a large high-frequency shift of the corresponding NMR line (A) as a consequence; while the position of the band due to the vinylic protons (B) suffers only a little shift due to lack of such interaction (i.e., assignments of the bands for A and B protons are reversed in the aliphatic solvent from those in the aromatic solvent). It should be clear from our above discussion that these authors altogether ignored the possibility of /c112 -/c112 self-stacking of MEH-PPV backbone segments in aliphatic solvents, which is the principal mode of organization in the condensed phase of PPVs. 18,19,23 As to our argument, if such stacking does occur in CDCl3 solutions, it is more likely that the resonance of the B protons would be equally affected by changing to an aromatic solvent. As a confirmation we took NMR spectra of MEH-PPV in mixed solvent-systems containing CDCl3 and an aromatic component over the whole composition range to look for any admixing and subsequent reversal of the signals A and B. The results of this study for the CDCl3-C6D5Cl system are shown in Fig. 2. It is clearly observed that as we decrease the volume fraction of the co-solvent C6D5Cl, resonance bands from both the A and the B protons linearly shift to lower frequencies; there is no sign of intermediate mixing and subsequent reversal of the bands. As the volume fraction of the aliphatic component in the solvent mixture is increased, polymer-solvent /c112 -/c112 interactions are gradually replaced by the stronger intersegment /c112 -/c112 stacking, and hence both of the resonance bands A and B gradually move towards lower-frequencies. Similar results have been observed for the CDCl3-C6D6 mixed-solvent system. It is therefore reasonable to retain the original assignments of the bands A and B in toluene solution due to the phenylic and vinylic backbone protons of MEH-PPV, respectively, to the corresponding bands in the aliphatic solvent, CDCl3.

The solutions of /c112 -conjugated polymers are complex systems composed of a dynamic component that has been described as a transient network of polymer chains from which the solution NMR signals of the polymer are obtained and an aggregate component from which the NMR signals are lost in the baseline due to the lack of motional narrowing on segmental arrest. 3,4,24 The aggregate component may even propagate throughout the solution on long standing leading to the formation of a gel-like jammed phase. 25,26 A careful examination of Fig. 2 would reveal that the intensities of the MEH-PPV backbone proton resonances are higher for solutions with intermediate co-solvent compositions. Very interesting results come out as we plot the normalized integrated intensities of the polymer proton signals in the mixed solvents as a function of the solvent composition as shown in Fig. 3. The backbone aromatic proton signals are reduced to as low as 44% in chlorobenzene-d5-rich solvents and to 57% in chloroform-d. Such a huge loss of motional narrowing indicates a high degree of aggregation of the polymer in both the solvents. A greater loss of motional narrowing due to aggregation in chlorobenzene-d5 rich solvents than in chloroform-d is expected because the aromatics are poorer solvent for MEHPPV than chloroform. 22 The sidechain alkyl proton signals show much higher intensity than the main chain protons and they remain higher in chloroform-d-rich solvents than the corresponding chlorobenzene-d5-rich compositions. This is because chloroform solvates the polymer sidechains better than chlorobenzene. Relatively lower intensity of the alkoxy proton signals than the alkyl ones may be attributed to the loss of their motional narrowing as a result of the nearness of their attachment to the aggregating chain backbone. However, the signal intensities gradually increase and pass through a maximum in the intermediate co-solvent compositions. This behavior may be related to the difference in the nature of the aggregation of the MEH-PPV

chains in aromatic and aliphatic solvents. In the aromatic solvent, the solvent molecules can compete with the polymer against polymer self-aggregation and the former being the predominant species, aggregation of the polymer-solvent /c112 -/c112 complex is favorable. On the other hand in chloroform-d, due to the absence of the polymer backbone-solvent interaction, polymer-polymer /c112 -/c112 stacking can lead to the aggregation. As we increase the chloroform-d fraction in the solvent mixture, it pulls apart the polymer chains of the prevalent polymer-solvent aggregates by solvating the side-chains of the polymer thus increasing motional narrowing. At higher volume fractions of the co-solvent CDCl3, the abundant aliphatic solvent species replaces the aromatic solvent. As CDCl3 can not solvate the backbone /c112 -system, desolvation occurs that leads to the formation of aggregates through polymer-polymer /c112 -/c112 stacking and consequent loss of motional narrowing of the NMR signals.
