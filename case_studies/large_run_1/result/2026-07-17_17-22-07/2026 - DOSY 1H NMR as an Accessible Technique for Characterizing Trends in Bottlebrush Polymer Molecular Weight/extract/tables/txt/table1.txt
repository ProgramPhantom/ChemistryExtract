To establish comparative benchmarks, PDMS homopolymer brushes with 1 kDa side chains were synthesized using the second-generation Grubbs Catalyst (G2), affording four polymers with M w ranging from 120 to 3040 kDa ( n bb between 70 and 1840) (Figure 2). Higher dispersities ( D = 1.5) were generally observed in these samples due to the slower initiation of G2 compared to its third-generation counterpart (G3) 60 and due to catalyst deactivation over long reaction times. 61 Another series of PDMS homopolymer brushes was synthesized using 5 kDa NPDMS macromonomers. These polymerizations were performed in an Arfilled glovebox and utilized G3 to produce 5 samples with M w ranging from 100 to 4000 kDa. These samples had lower dispersities due to the fast initiation of G3, although slight shoulders are apparent in the sample GPC chromatograms. We attribute this high-molecular-weight deviation to the presence of small amounts of macromonomers bearing two norbornene end groups, 62,63 causing the formation of minute quantities of dimerized bottlebrushes. However, these impurities make up a minority fraction of the total sample, and thus we assume have little impact on the bottlebrush diffusive behavior.

In addition to the PDMS BBPs, we synthesized BBCPs and bottlebrush statistical copolymers (BSCPs). The BBCPs contained 5 kDa PDMS and 5 kDa PEG side chains, while the BSCPs used 1 kDa PDMS and 2 kDa PEG macromonomers. ROMP of these macromonomers using G3 produced BBCPs in the range of 290 kDa to 3.5 MDa with high conversion and generally low dispersity ( D &lt;1.2). It should be noted that small amounts of the nonfunctionalized side chain ( e.g. , hydroxy-terminated PEG or PDMS) were present in the final product corresponding to the peak seen at 16.5 min in the GPC chromatogram. However, this peak represents a small (&lt;6%) amount of the final product (Figure S4), and we do not expect it to substantially influence the diffusive behavior of the BBCPs. Lastly, the BSCPs were synthesized with G3 using a Schlenk apparatus to afford a set of four polymers with M w ranging from 50 to 480 kDa and low dispersities ( D &lt;1.1). High monomer conversion was achieved in all cases, and the desired M w and dispersities aligned with the expected values. A summary of the synthesized polymers is given in Table 1.

Following polymerization, all samples were subjected to MALS-GPC to evaluate M w, followed by DOSY at 1 mg/mL in CDCl3 by utilizing a Bruker AVANCE III 400 MHz NMR spectrometer. The 1 H resonance frequency was set to 400.13 MHz in a 5 mm broadband-observe Prodigy cryoprobe, 3 mm NMR tubes, a probe temperature of 293 K, and a stimulated echo pulse sequence with a bipolar gradient. The methyl 1 H signal of the PDMS side chains (0.09 ppm) was used to track diffusive behavior, as this signal is present on all polymer

̵

samples and is notably sharp/distinct. All signal decay curves were fit using the Bruker Dynamics Center software, and an example analysis can be found in the Supporting Information (Figures S1 and S2). Specifically, a two-component fit was used to negate diffusive effects from residual unfunctionalized PDMS or PEG present following the esterification step. We note that care should be taken such that DOSY samples are at concentrations lower than the overlap concentration of synthesized bottlebrushes. As described by models and simulations by Borisov and co-workers, 64,65 bottlebrush polymers in dilute solution exhibit a persistence length that scales linearly with the bottlebrush cross section, allowing the estimation of a sample's R g (and thus overlap concentration) from variables such as side chain length and grafting density. Utilizing these models shows good agreement with the measured values of R h . The reader is encouraged to refer to Figure S5 for details on the equations utilized.
