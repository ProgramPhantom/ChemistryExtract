Before investigating the bottlebrush polymer molecular weights by DOSY, it is important to understand the information that the derived scaling relationships provide. Namely, the scaling relationships seen in these types of experiments can inform us about the 'quality' of solvent used in these systems as they pertain to enthalpic interactions. Theta solvents are described as solvents of sufficient quality such that two-body interactions ( i.e. , monomer -monomer-excluded volume interactions) appear to be screened, or nonexistent. 31,66 This means that the polymer's characteristic length ( R g, R h ) scales with the molecular weight to the 0.50 power. Importantly, though, theta solvents are not enthalpically neutral to the polymer  there still lies some, albeit small, penalty for having the polymer chain surrounded by the solvent molecules ( i.e. , χ ≠ 0). 31,66 When the polymer samples have a solvent to which it is truly enthalpically neutral (meaning that the monomers in the polymer chain have no preference of having an adjacent monomer or a solvent molecule next to it), the polymer is in an a thermal solvent. Thus, the polymer will attempt to form a truly random walk, but since monomer units cannot occupy the same space, the polymer chain is slightly swollen, meaning that it will scale with M w to 0.588, better known as the Flory exponent. 31 These distinctions are important to note as the bottlebrush architecture introduces additional entropic considerations not found in linear polymers, meaning that scaling relationships derived for linear polymers cannot necessarily be directly translated to their bottlebrush counterparts.
