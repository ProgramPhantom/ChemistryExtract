In all cases, the methodology described yielded average error rates below 4 %, underscoring its accuracy and consistency in determining molecular weights across various polymer types and solvent environments. This conservative estimation highlights the reliability of diffusion NMR as a complementary technique to SEC. The molecular weights obtained using this methodology closely align with those determined by SEC (Table 1), with deviations typically within 1 -4 %. These differences can be attributed to the inherent variations in the principles underlying the  two  techniques:  SEC  relies  on  calibration  standards  and  column separations, while diffusion NMR directly measures molecular motion in solution. SEC provides detailed molecular weight distributions and is ideal for routine analysis but requires calibration standards, consumables,  and  regular  maintenance,  making  it  less  suitable  for  complex polymers  or  solvent-sensitive  systems.  Diffusion  NMR,  on  the  other hand,  uses  diffusion  coefficients  to  infer  molecular  weight  without external calibration and excels in solvent flexibility and complex polymer analysis. Although the dataset focuses on a subset of the polymers listed  in  Table  1,  this  selection  represents  a  proof  of  concept  for  the diffusion NMR methodology. The chosen polymers span a wide range of molecular weights and were tested in multiple solvent environments and different  concentrations  to  validate  the  applicability  of  the  method under diverse conditions. Recent advancements, such as ultrafast (UF) and  time-resolved  (TR)  diffusion  NMR  methods,  have  significantly reduced acquisition times, making the technique more competitive with SEC in terms of speed. Ongoing efforts are focused on adapting these methods for faster and more accurate polymer molecular weight predictions,  potentially  bridging  the  gap  between  the  two  techniques. Future work will expand the dataset to include additional polymers, further generalizing the approach and solidifying its role as a practical and efficient tool for polymer analysis.

## 4. Conclusions

An innovative iterative methodology for accurately estimating the F.M. Arrabal-Campos et al.

molecular weight of linear and narrow PPG and PS polymers has been described for the first time using both PGSE and SILT-DOSY NMR procedures. The main inputs to this method are the quantitative diffusion coefficient (D), solvent viscosity, and sample concentration. The method has been demonstrated to be valid in a wide range of concentrations, thanks to the developed Kappa and high dilution curves. These curves capture the growing polymeric interactions that occur as a function of concentration,  helping  to  deduce  true  molecular  weight  at  any  concentration and in any deuterated solvent. The molecular weight predictions obtained by this method has achieved an absolute error below 4 % in all the cases, indicating its high accuracy and effectiveness. This new iterative method is presented in the Supporting Information as Code S1 and Code S2. Current research efforts are focused on exploring the broader  application  of  this  method  to  other  polymers  with  higher polydispersity  and  especially  those  based  on  polysaccharides.  Additionally,  we  are  investigating  the  implications  of  the  transitions observed in the Kappa plot, which likely reflect shifts in polymer dynamics  due  to  excluded  volume  effects  and  mean-field  interactions. These transitions hold potential for providing deeper insights into chain behavior, entanglements, and polymer -solvent interactions.
